# Model card: RiskDesk

Version 0.1.0 · Prepared for a model-risk review of a portfolio project · Synthetic data only

## 1. Purpose
RiskDesk computes one-day market-risk measures for a synthetic trading book and uses LLM-based agents to investigate limit breaches and draft notes. The agents explain and route cases. They never compute risk numbers and never change limits or positions.

## 2. Users and intended use
- Market-risk analysts reviewing synthetic breach cases.
- Risk managers approving or rejecting queued reports.
- Model-risk reviewers testing the controls.

Intended use: demonstration and teaching, and a template for a controlled pilot on synthetic data.

## 3. Out-of-scope use
- Any use on real positions, real market data or real counterparties.
- Any automatic action on limits, positions or client communications.
- Any use of the drafting models for decisions without a named human reviewer.
- Regulatory reporting or capital calculation.

## 4. Components
| Component | Type | Produces numbers? |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Risk engine (`riskdesk/engine`) | Deterministic calculation: pricing, historical, parametric and Monte Carlo VaR, ES, component VaR, stress, backtest | Yes (the only source of numbers) |
| Limit framework | Rules over engine outputs | No (compares engine outputs) |
| Investigation graph (`riskdesk/agents/graph.py`) | Workflow with a template or local LLM drafter | No |
| Drafters | Template (deterministic) or Ollama `qwen2.5:0.5b` (local, free) | No (text only, checked by the grounding gate) |
| Retrieval (`riskdesk/rag`) | BM25 plus character-trigram vectors, fused by rank | No |

## 5. Inputs
- Synthetic daily factor moves: 750 days for 9 spot factors and 6 implied-vol factors, generated from seed 42 with fat tails and two injected stress episodes.
- A synthetic book of 40 positions on three desks (6 cash equities, 22 equity options, 12 FX forwards), seed 7.
- Calibrated limits in `data/limits.json`: hard limit set at 1.5 times the baseline measure.
- A knowledge base of 4 policy and reference files and 50 synthetic incident logs.

## 6. Outputs
- Desk-level VaR, ES, stress loss, vega and limit utilisation.
- Breach investigation reports (Pydantic-validated) placed in an approval queue.
- Daily VaR commentary queued for approval.

## 7. Known limitations
1. Synthetic data only. The factor distributions, correlations and stress episodes are assumptions, not calibrated to any market.
2. The parametric and Monte Carlo measures are normal approximations. For the equity desks the historical VaR is higher than the normal measures, because the 250-day window includes a fat-tailed stress episode.
3. Stress scenarios are analogues named after historical episodes. They are not replays of those episodes.
4. Attribution uses a linear (Euler) decomposition. It is accurate for linear books and approximate for option books with large gamma.
5. Vol and spot factors are treated as jointly normal in the parametric model.
6. Regulatory summaries in the knowledge base are paraphrases. The SS1/23 and SR 26-2 points were checked on 12 November 2026 against the Bank of England and Federal Reserve texts. The 97.5% ES level was checked in MAR33.3 of the primary PDF. The Basel traffic-light zones were checked only against search results.
7. The retrieval evaluation (40 questions) was written with wording close to the document headings, so recall@5 = 1.0 overstates real performance.
8. The local drafting model is unreliable on numbers: see the validation report.
9. The golden set is generated by the same engine it tests. Scoring is objective with respect to the injected cause, but the injections are designed to be easy to detect.

## 8. Ethical and privacy notes
No personal data is used. Names in the PII filter (traders, counterparties) are invented for the demonstration. The PII filter is a control, not a guarantee.
